# How Many Sheets of Drywall Do You Need for a Room?

Buying drywall is one of those jobs where a small math mistake means an extra trip to the store. The good news is that the estimate takes four steps.

## Step 1: Wall area

Add up the length of every wall (the perimeter) and multiply by the ceiling height. A 12 by 10 foot room with 8 foot ceilings has a perimeter of 44 feet, so the wall area is 44 x 8 = 352 square feet.

## Step 2: Subtract openings

Take away doors and windows. A standard door is about 21 square feet (3 x 7 feet) and a small window about 12 square feet (3 x 4 feet). That leaves 352 - 21 - 12 = 319 square feet of wall to cover.

## Step 3: Add waste

Cuts around outlets, corners and openings mean you should order extra. Ten percent is a common allowance, which brings the total to about 351 square feet.

## Step 4: Divide by the sheet size

A 4 x 8 foot sheet covers 32 square feet. So 351 / 32 = about 11 sheets for the walls. The same method works for the ceiling: a 12 x 10 foot ceiling is 120 square feet, or about 5 sheets once you add waste and round up.
